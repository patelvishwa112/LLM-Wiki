# The Antithesis Principle

## Key Takeaways

- Many truths about human nature can be aimed **outward** (tactic: be effective with others) or **inward** (antithesis: eliminate that default in yourself).
- Smart people usually stop at the outward tactic. Wise people also take the inward path.
- Examples of the split:
  - Learning needs entertainment → make teaching engaging **and** train yourself to learn from “boring” sources (e.g. *The Courage to Be Disliked*, *Understanding Michael Porter*, *The Mom Test*).
  - Charisma wins influence → build charisma **and** stop being swayed by others’ charisma (most people who think they’re immune are not).
  - First ~7 seconds shape impressions → craft first impressions **and** refuse fixed judgments of others from those seconds.
  - Great managers matter → become a good manager **and** become someone who does excellent work under average or absent managers.
  - People love analogies → explain *with* evocative analogies; do **not** reason *by* them when deciding.
- Why the inward half is rare: love of consistency (antithesis feels contradictory) + unexamined default programming validated by peers.
- Core line: **Smart ≠ Wise.** Outward = smart; inward antithesis = wise.

## Why It Matters

A compact filter for any “people work this way” observation: harvest the tactic *and* name the self-default to kill. Useful for leadership, product judgment, career agency (manager quality), and epistemic hygiene (analogy-as-persuasion vs analogy-as-reason).
