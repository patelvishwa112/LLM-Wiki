# How to keep thinking

Sean Goedecke (2026-08-07): 2026 eng work feels like a game show of rapid judgment cards (schema ok? tests real? architecture smell? which of three AI impls?). Frontier models → spin agents, **context-switch across results** (often many sessions on **one** task: explore / multi-impl / review / manual test). Skillful, not mindless — but short on hammock time.

## Why not just slow down?

- Close-reading LLM slop all day is miserable; skim-for-nuggets hurts less.  
- Competitive pressure: if peers have a 10× button, not pressing it is career risk (broader post-2010s frenetic tech, not only LLMs).  
- Fear isn’t “brain melt” papers — it’s **bias toward skimming/judging** and away from slow creativity.

## Practice: write and read (yourself)

1. **Write in your own words** — LLM-assisted drafting doesn’t count for this goal even with heavy iteration. Articulating forces thinking; “ideas” in the head are usually directional fragments until written. Pushes back on “ideas are easy, execution is everything.”  
2. **Read dense non-fiction books slowly** — antithesis of AI slop; brain craves density.  
3. **Combine**: book → hard thought → often more books → write what you learned (book reports).

## Don’t lose the habit

Slow reflection may leave the **job** more than the **profession**. Outside-work practice still matters. At work, total loss is a mistake: e.g. large refactors on complex codebases — models can be low-error but not yet **tasteful**; some problems still need a full human pass.

## Why it matters

Names the agentic-era cognitive risk cleanly and offers a portable counter-habit (own writing + books). Complements visual anti-prose skills (/show-me), FDE productization, and clear-thinking notes — different angle: protect slow mode, not just speed review.

## Skeptical read

Personal essay; “miserable to close-read” is taste. Competitive framing can justify never slowing. Still a useful protocol if you feel skim-mode atrophy.
