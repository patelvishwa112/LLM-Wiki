# Your Entire Life Changes When You Realize Getting Rich Is A Skill Issue

**Source:** Devon Canup, 2026-09-30. A long-form X Article (~1.9M views) pitching a six-part framework for getting rich through skill acquisition and identity change rather than saving, ladder-climbing, or manifestation.

Canup's thesis is that wealth is a competence problem, not a luck or privilege problem. He names the target state "unconsciously rich": a point where income stops requiring conscious effort because the operator has automated the few actions that actually generate revenue. He frames the common dismissal of wealthy people — scam, nepotism, daddy's money — as cope, an ego defense that keeps people from studying what the money-makers actually do.

The argument runs in three layers. First, **vehicle selection**: he dismisses "slow rich" advice — $500/month into the S&P 500 for 40 years, corporate ladder-climbing, manifestation — and insists the constraint is usually the vehicle, not the effort. He lists cash-flowing options (YouTube, influencing, marketing agency, affiliate, trading/crypto, "boring" businesses like landscaping) and cites friends who reached multiple millions in landscaping over roughly seven years. Second, **feedback velocity**: a four-year degree gives you one slow feedback cycle, while publishing gives you hundreds. His loop is inputs → process → outputs → feedback, with reps as the raw material for pattern recognition ("reps = data points = pattern = money"). He maps money skill onto the Dunning-Kruger curve — unconsciously incompetent, consciously incompetent, consciously competent, unconsciously competent — and claims the second stage is where most people quit.

Third, **identity**: he coins "Personality LARPing" — deliberately acting as the person you want to be — and grounds it in Daryl Bem's 1972 self-perception theory ("you decide who you are by watching what you do"), so identity precedes beliefs and actions rather than following them. He pairs that with environment change: Wendy Wood's finding that ~45% of daily behavior is repeated in the same place, and a University of Bath nudge study that only worked on people who had moved house in the previous three months (his "90-day window"). The prescription is to disappear for six months and lock in.

Read it as a framework, not evidence. Every financial figure — $10M+ made, multiple $100K days, $500K spent on courses and mentors, 4,000+ videos, 20,000 posts — is self-reported and unaudited, and the piece is a funnel to a free newsletter and a free "faceless YouTube" training. Its genuinely transferable content is structural: choosing vehicles with tight feedback loops, treating skill as a stack of separately trainable sub-skills, and using identity/environment design to make repetition cheaper.

## Why it matters

For anyone building AI systems, the load-bearing idea is the feedback-loop argument: the advantage of these vehicles is reps-per-unit-time, and AI compresses exactly that ratio — more experiments, faster evidence, more data points per week. The skill decomposition (copywriting, packaging, teaching, sales, management) is also a useful map for where models already substitute and where human reps still compound. For investors, the "vehicle, not effort" framing is a reminder that returns concentrate in markets with fast feedback and low capital intensity, not in slow, credential-gated ladders — while the self-reported numbers should be treated as marketing, not as a track record.

## Key points

- Core claim: "getting rich is a skill issue" — you are at one of four Dunning-Kruger stages of money competence, and the goal is stage four (unconsciously competent).
- Rejects "slow rich": $500/month into the S&P 500 for 40 years, ignoring inflation and the 40 years of life spent waiting.
- Vehicle list: YouTube (his first $1M), influencing, marketing agency, affiliate, day trading/crypto, and "boring" businesses — landscaping friends at multiple millions/year after ~7 years.
- Feedback loop model: inputs → process → outputs → feedback; ~100 articles in 2-3 months is framed as more marketing evidence than a 4-year degree.
- "Reps = data points = pattern = money." Concrete reps he cites: 4,000+ YouTube videos, 20,000 social posts, thousands of sales calls.
- Daryl Bem, self-perception theory (1972): identity is inferred from observed behavior, so identity → beliefs → actions, not the reverse.
- Wendy Wood: ~45% of daily behavior repeats in the same location; University of Bath nudges only worked on people who moved within the prior ~3 months.
- Investment claim: at least $500K personally spent on courses/mentors; one ~$100 Udemy copywriting course ("writing with flair") called out as high ROI.
- Self-reported outcomes: 27 years old, $10M+ online, multiple $100K days, 3x income growth over ~18 months of full-time travel.
