# Why You Waste Your Evenings: Post-Work Fatigue and Prefrontal Glutamate

**Source:** Dr. Dominic Ng (@DrDominicNg), 2026-02-03. A long-form neuroscience explainer for a general audience, reposted from his newsletter (Brain Health Decoded), with a paid-subscriber follow-up behind a Substack link.

## Synthesis

The post's argument is that the familiar evening collapse — sofa, phone, mental rehearsal of tasks you are not doing — is not a character defect but a metabolic one. Every act of focus, distraction-resistance, or judgment call recruits the prefrontal cortex, and the author frames each recruitment as carrying a cost that accumulates over a workday rather than resetting between meetings. His evidence is a 2022 Current Biology study from the Paris Brain Institute in which two groups did the same task types for roughly six hours, one at low and one at high cognitive demand. Only the hard-task group showed a steadily rising glutamate concentration in the lateral prefrontal cortex across the day.

Glutamate is the main excitatory neurotransmitter, so the claim is not that it is bad — it is that local accumulation outpaces clearance during sustained demand, which makes that region harder to activate and pushes behaviour toward the cheapest available option. In the study, the depleted group shifted toward immediate, low-effort rewards and away from harder-but-better choices. The author maps that directly onto the evening: the phone instead of the run, the scroll instead of the book. His analogy is lactic acid in a muscle, and he is explicit that this is an analogy rather than a mechanism claim.

The prescription follows from the diagnosis: recovery means taking the prefrontal cortex offline, not resting the body. Walking without a podcast, music you already know, a recipe you have cooked before, sitting outside doing nothing — the common property is no decisions, no judgment, no impulse control, with a suggested 20–30 minute window before the evening begins. His explanation for why "just push through" fails is the sharpest part of the post: willpower is itself a prefrontal function, so forcing yourself asks the depleted system to repair itself with the resource it has already spent — which is why the same advice works at 9 AM and collapses at 8 PM.

Caveats worth keeping: this is popular science with a newsletter and paid-post funnel at the end, the study is a single controlled experiment rather than a replication base, and the jump from one workday of measured glutamate to an evening of Netflix is the author's inference. The practical advice is cheap and low-risk, so it survives the weaker evidence even if the mechanism story is tidier than the literature.

## Why it matters

Most people building or operating AI systems are doing the highest-decision-density work of their lives — reviewing agent output, adjudicating evals, holding many open judgment calls — which is exactly the workload this post says depletes fastest. If evening capacity is a metabolic budget rather than a moral one, then scheduling deep review work late, or expecting good judgment from yourself after a day of agent supervision, is a design error. It also reframes human-in-the-loop placement: an exhausted reviewer approving agent actions is a predictable quality failure, not a discipline problem.

## Key points

- Elevates one claim: hard cognitive work raises glutamate concentration in the lateral prefrontal cortex over a ~6-hour simulated workday; the low-demand group showed no such rise (2022 Current Biology study, Paris Brain Institute).
- The consequence measured in the study was a shift toward immediate, easy rewards and away from harder-but-better options — effort avoidance, not laziness.
- Recovery is defined as prefrontal idleness, not physical rest: body may be active, decisions must not be required.
- Proposed dose: 20–30 minutes of low-decision activity before the evening starts, so the reward environment you wanted becomes accessible again.
- Willpower is treated as a prefrontal function, so "push through" is self-repair by an already-spent system — hence the morning/evening asymmetry.
- Concrete recovery examples given: walk without a podcast, familiar music, a recipe already known, sitting outside doing nothing.
- Author is explicit that the muscle-lactate comparison is an analogy, and the post ends with a paid-Substack offer of four strategies for working with a depleted brain.
