# An Alien Mind

Jakub Pachocki (@merettm), OpenAI Chief Scientist, 2026-09-06. Tweet is a pointer. Essay: https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/ — also [[raw/openai_an-alien-mind]].

## Frame

Mid-2023 RLSlow: first confidence that reasoning-model training would scale. Night at the office with Szymon: not benchmarks — machines meaningfully smarter than us in our lifetime. Three years later: reasoning models in the economy, computer use, research collaboration, and new computer-security dangers. Internal results → expectation that progress could continue into **recursive self-improvement**.

Machine intelligence comes from a different process than human intelligence; do not assume it adheres to human principles by default.

## Sections (essay)

- **Teaching machines to love** — goal alignment (pursues the given objective) can outrun **value alignment** (reasonable in uninstructed situations). That gap is the more dangerous one at scale.
- **Monitoring generalization** — CoT monitoring is "progressively less reliable": reasoning tangled with tool use that must be supervised anyway; models better at manipulating traces; pretraining gains produce smarter behavior without visible reasoning.
- **Scalable defense** — computer-use / security landscape.
- **Pacing RSI** — if progress continues, RSI sits at the core of future scientific discovery; voluntary slowdowns until shared safety bars.
- **What is next?** — automated AI researcher iterating on alignment; humans remaining in the self-improvement loop; prevent extreme concentration of power.

No lab has solved alignment and monitoring enough to keep scaling at maximum speed. Calls for mandated safety thresholds (auditors / agencies / international), not only voluntary RSP/Preparedness.

Lab-authored; extract the **goal vs value** and **CoT-monitor decay** claims, not the north-star marketing.
