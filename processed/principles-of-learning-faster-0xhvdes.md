# The Principles of Learning Faster

Hades (@0xHvdes, 2026-08-30). Essay: more consumption ≠ more learning. Learning is a meta-skill. Seven principles:

1. **Learn by doing** — tutorials feel like progress; action creates the real problems that design the next lesson. Ask: what can I do with what I already know?
2. **Shorten the feedback loop** — ten unpublished drafts ≠ ten published ones with reader drop-off. Practice without feedback rehearses the mistake. Ask how fast you can find out you’re wrong.
3. **Struggle before you search** — Google/ChatGPT/Claude skip the hypothesis. A few minutes of trying first makes the eventual answer stick. Getting the answer faster ≠ learning faster.
4. **Learn just in time** — don’t prep every adjacent skill first. Start the project; let the immediate problem write the curriculum.
5. **Retrieval beats recognition** — close the book and explain three ideas. Familiarity is an illusion of knowledge.
6. **Connect before you collect** — bookmarks ≠ understanding. Value is links (psychology → marketing → sales → distribution).
7. **Learning compounds** — later ideas land on a denser graph; experienced founders parse a business model faster because they have more hooks, not more IQ.

Punchline: turn information → understanding → action until learning itself gets easier. “Get better at becoming better.”

## Why it matters

Principle III is the AI-era one: paste-into-Claude as default skips the struggle that teaches. VI is the wiki argument (graph over pile). Complements Claude study-systems notes (tactics) with a process layer (do / feedback / JIT).

## Skeptical read

Generic creator essay, no citations. The seven items are standard (deliberate practice, retrieval, just-in-time). Useful as a compact checklist, not new science. Heart-emoji close, no course funnel.
