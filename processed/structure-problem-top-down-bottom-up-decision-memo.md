# Structure-problem: top-down + bottom-up decision memos

@nurijanian (prodmgmt.world) on turning complex PM problems into shippable directives via a dual-path decision memo — not just Pyramid "answer first."

## Method

1. **State the decision** — question + approver + deadline (not a topic label like "Pricing").
2. **Top-down first (no data)** — list the few logically possible answers under ideal conditions; protects objectivity.
3. **Bottom-up evidence** — group sources (tickets, user convos, usage, sales notes, standup comments); let each group speak; do not force-fit a preferred conclusion.
4. **One-page synthesis** — side-by-side top-down vs bottom-up: alignments, conflicts, options with no evidence.
5. **Conclusion first + kill condition** — decision in sentence one; evidence below; name remaining disagreements; one sentence on what evidence would reverse the call.

## Beyond Pyramid Principle

Pyramid often stops at answer-first packaging. The load-bearing work is **steps 2–4**: run both analyses **independently**, then surface discrepancies. Answer-first without that pass is just predetermined outcome theater.

## Why it's hard

- Tools (and AI) keep subdividing → complexity grows with no stop signal for closure.
- Judging "consistent enough to decide" vs "still reconciling" is human, not process-automatic.
- PMs often already have the judgment; they lack a **written format** that makes it legible.

## AI angle

More drafts, breakdowns, and options → harder mental synthesis of detail vs summary. Blank templates don't stop summary-override-at-midnight. An agent can **reliably** run both passes, highlight agree/disagree, emit summary-first page; humans keep the meeting.
