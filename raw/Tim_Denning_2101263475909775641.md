It’s easy for you to get fooled over and over again.
That’s what it means to be human.
You try to achieve the same goal multiple times and fall short. No matter what you do the result doesn’t seem to change. It’s frustrating as hell…
And it’s why a lot of people give up.
There’s a simple solution though: Master pattern recognition. Learn to see the patterns in everything, so you can exploit them for your benefit. One of the greatest teachers of our time Tony Robbins says “There are certain patterns that are universal.”
Tony first saw this when he wanted to write a book about investing. Even though Tony has never worked on Wall Street or been a hedge fund manager, he interviewed ten of the greatest minds in the finance industry. He studied them.
By the end of writing his first book on investing, he saw patterns that all of these greats were doing without realizing it. That book is called “Money: Master the Game.”
That one book quietly changed the trajectory of my life — but not in the way you'd guess. I'll show you exactly how later.
Pattern recognition is a skill that gives you an unfair advantage. It lets you enter a new field, or go from being a beginner to a master much faster. Every skill, career, goal, or business can be figured out with the hidden skill of pattern recognition.
Mastering pattern recognition will change your life forever.
The ice cream truck driver who saw the pattern everyone else called "random"
In 1984, Michael Larson was an unemployed ice cream truck driver in Ohio.
No fancy degree. No millions in the bank. No talent anyone could point to. Just a fat man, a couch, and a VCR.
Like a loser, he got hooked on a CBS game show called Press Your Luck. The setup was simple: contestants stopped a board of flashing lights to land on cash, while praying they didn’t hit the “Whammy”.
Everybody assumed the board was random.
Everybody except Michael.
While the rest of America watched the show with a beer in hand, Larson recorded every episode and did something psychotic. He sat there with the remote and paused the tape. Frame. By. Frame. For weeks. Like a fat caveman version of a data scientist, hunched over a TV, hunting for something nobody believed was there.
And he found it.
The “random” board wasn’t random at all. It cycled through just five repeating patterns. Five. That was the whole secret.
He also figured out that two specific squares never held a Whammy and handed you a bonus spin every time. If he could hit them on command, he could run the board basically forever.
So he memorized all five patterns and drilled his timing until he could stop a spinning board on the exact square he wanted, on cue, like a sniper. Then he spent all his ice cream truck savings on a flight to LA to audition for the TV show.
A contestant supervisor was suspicious and didn’t want to let him on, but a producer overruled him. Michael looked like a harmless hillbilly from Tennessee.
Michael got on stage and wowed the audience.
He did dozens of spins in a row and didn’t get a single Whammy. The audience lost it. The host got twitchy. In the control room, CBS executives watched in slow-motion horror as this random ice cream truck guy calmly dismantled their show.
When Michael finally stopped, he’d won $110,237. It was the biggest one-day haul in game show history at the time.
CBS was pissed. They refused to pay.
They launched an investigation and studied the tapes for cheating.
And found nothing. Because there was no rule against paying attention. They paid him every cent, quietly reprogrammed the board, and buried the episode for nearly two decades so nobody would get any ideas.
An unemployed man with a VCR beat a national television network at its own game. Not with money. Not with connections. Not with luck.
With a pattern everyone else was too lazy to see.
The 5th-grader explanation of pattern recognition (so you can actually use it)
Pattern recognition is your brain’s ability to see the invisible structure underneath the noise.
Where everyone else sees chaos, randomness, or a pile of unrelated facts, you see a shape you’ve seen before. And you instantly know what it means and what’s coming next.
Here’s the part that’ll mess with you: pattern recognition is not intelligence. It’s not a gift. It’s not some magic third eye you’re born with.
Pattern recognition is compressed experience.
Every “intuitive” genius you’ve ever admired is really just running a massive database in the background.
Take chess. Sit a grandmaster in front of a board mid-game, let them look for 5 seconds, then take it away. They can rebuild the entire position from memory, almost perfectly.
Superhuman, right?
Except do the same thing with pieces scattered randomly across the board — a position that could never happen in a real game — and the grandmaster does no better than you or me.
Read that again.
Their “photographic memory” vanishes the second the pattern disappears.
Because they were never memorizing pieces. They were seeing familiar shapes, formations they’d seen 10,000 times before. Where a beginner sees 25 individual pieces, the master sees 5 or 6 meaningful groups. Same board.
Completely different resolution.
That’s the whole thing. Pattern recognition is what turns 10,000 hours of experience into a single glance. It feels like intuition. It’s actually a library. And once you understand that, the good news hits you: a library is something you can build.
The quiet pattern that changed my life, made me a millionaire, and allowed me to quit banking forever
I want to share a secret with you. I’ve never shared this before, so allow me to be vulnerable.
On the outside, I look like some lucky Aussie that won the internet lottery, got millions of followers, and retired on a beach with a hot wife and 2 kids.
This is a lie.
I hinted that pattern recognition changed my life. Let me share how. Before, I told you the Tony Robbins book in 2014 helped me see patterns. After I finished the book I became high as a kite on the idea of pattern recognition.
I felt euphoric. I felt like I’d opened Pandora’s box.
I thought to myself that people spend their entire life to make money. It occupies so much of their time. But if money truly was nothing more than a game full of patterns, then if I, Timothy Denning, could master it, perhaps I could win too.
I realize what I just said sounds delusional but that’s how I thought. I spent the next few years reading every book about money and Wall Street to recognize the patterns.
One night I found a glitch. Here it is…
Every one of us gets paid in a currency issued by a government. I studied history and noted that time and time again every empire in existence exploited its currency. It was too tempting not to. So if every government in history will always destroy their currency, then what does that say about money?
I realized my entire life I worked my butt off to earn this currency. I traded my precious time for it. So understanding the pattern of money had a huge payoff.
And I found the pattern.
Every government creates money from nothing through money printing. The more they print money the more all assets/investments rise in price. So as long as I invested money in the right things, the rise in money supply would inflate my investments.
I saw a pattern that some investments got inflated more than others. But no matter what, the pattern was obvious. From 2014 onwards I exploited this pattern, and it made me enough money to never work again.
Sure, I had some business success. I built a following. But the pattern that actually saved my life was understanding the pattern of how money is created.
If you’re willing to find the pattern, you can literally crack the code to any wild goal, outcome, or business.
The most important pattern in the history of the world
Here’s the pattern that runs the world:
Good times create weak people.
Weak people create bad times.
Bad times create strong people.
Strong people create good times.
Once you understand this pattern you can handle uncertainty like a Jedi master. You can face hard times in the middle of winter and know that bikini-babe summers are right around the corner.
This pattern gives you a quiet confidence. It’s one of the few patterns that explains the entire world in 4 sentences.
That’s how powerful patterns are.
They can take a complex topic like human history with infinite stories and data points, and compress them down to one 30-second insight. Mind blown? Mine is. Excuse me while I go to the dunny (joking not joking). Let the pattern of guaranteed good times fuel your optimism and help you achieve any goal.
You either see patterns, or you become a slave to subconscious patterns
It’s time to get a little dark.
This ain’t all lollipops and Kinder surprises. Karen in the comments needs to say “This Tim guy said he likes dead babies” before the real breakthrough happens. So here it is. Let’s get Karen in the comments fired up.
The best tactics are a paradox. Pattern recognition can change your life, but underneath, patterns can also ruin your life too.
Mastering pattern recognition did allow me to reach modern freedom. But failing to see patterns held me back for years before that. Some will choose not to see patterns. They will say pattern recognition is bullsh*t. But everything runs on patterns.
You either choose to see patterns and master the skill, or unconscious patterns will shape your life and you’ll call it bad luck, racism, discrimination, being neurodivergent, unhealed trauma, or some other BS.
Psychologist Carl Jung said it this way:
Until you make the unconscious conscious, it will direct your life and you will call it fate.
You must make the unconscious patterns conscious. This is simple but not easy. Most people are slaves to unconscious patterns.
Addictions are nothing more than unconscious patterns. You can stop jerking off to boobies or quit vaping if you really want to – but you won’t until you see the unconscious pattern.
For example, I had an addiction to junk food. I noticed whenever I felt anxious, I’d eat to relieve stress. So I took up the habit of meditation, became more chill, then I didn’t feel the need to down 7-Eleven slurpees by the gallon.
It wasn’t an eating problem. It was an anxiety problem.
When I faced extreme poverty I saw another pattern. Every time I found an opportunity, I sabotaged myself by focusing on all the downsides and being obsessed with risks instead of ROI.
This kept me stuck at $50K a year for longer than I care to admit.
On the surface, it looked like an earning problem. Or a generational issue I inherited because I came from a poor family. But it wasn’t.
It was a series of deeply limiting beliefs that made every million-dollar opportunity look like a brown bear looking for a human lunch and some hairy legs to munch.
The victim mindset is perhaps the greatest destroyer of human potential in existence.
When bad things happen or tragedy strikes, the unconscious pattern of becoming a victim takes over. Instead of “I can change this,” we go to “this happened to me and now I can’t live a good life again.”
One perspective is empowering, the other one is a prison sentence served in a pool full of quicksand surrounded by beer bros smoking cigarettes yelling “There’s no way out Timmy Boy!”
The neuroscience technique high performers have known about for 100s of years
I’m not a psychologist. I didn’t study neuroscience.
But when I first encountered Tony Robbins’ work I learned about pattern interrupts, and it’s the solution to breaking subconscious patterns that produce bad results.
The idea goes that once you can see a destructive pattern you can break it. For example, if someone has a stutter and they want to get rid of it, then every time they find themselves about to stutter, they can take a radically different action to break the pattern. Here’s how I used the technique…
Years ago I was petrified of public speaking. I spent several years going to Toastmasters to face my fear. I thought I had got good. Then my bank boss asked me to deliver a speech to a room full of 2000 bankers.
The audience would be made up of every leader I’d ever worked with. And every possible leader I might want to work with in the future. To top it off, days before, I’d found out I was going to lose my job because my team was being disbanded.
5 years of work down the drain.
These high stakes changed the game. Before, I was giving speeches to strangers so it was easier. But this audience felt like one mistake could get me fired and make me unemployed forever.
Right before the speech I nearly poo’d my pants. No joke. My hands were sweaty, I felt like I was going to faint, and I worried wee would gush out while I was on stage.
30 seconds before the speech, I had a panic attack.
I couldn’t do it.
That’s when I had to pull out the only tool I had left: a pattern interrupt. I walked up on stage in a panic. I looked for something to interrupt the pattern.
I saw a big, gold bell. Without hesitation I grabbed the bell and rang it so damn loudly the audience thought I’d lost my mind.
I started giggling like a school girl.
The audience smiled. That bell incident broke my pattern of panic and allowed me to chill out. I gave the speech of my life and got a standing ovation at the end.
In that moment the flow state and stream of consciousness was so strong, I felt superhuman. I went from panic to thought leader on a dime all because of a silly pattern interrupt.
Use pattern interrupts to break unconscious patterns.
How you quietly build the skill of pattern recognition
Nobody’s born with patterns pre-installed in their brain.
You can only recognize what you’ve seen before, which means the entire game is loading your brain with the right reps that let you see the right patterns. Here’s how.
1. Do extreme numbers of reps
You cannot recognize a pattern you’ve never encountered. Period.
The person who’s seen 1,000 sales calls reads a prospect in seconds. The amateur is guessing. So you need reps, and a lot of them.
But not passive reps.
Watching the same show for 20 years won’t do what Michael Larson did in a few weeks. Because he wasn’t watching. He was studying.
2. Chase feedback like your life depends on it.
This is the one everybody skips.
Experience only compounds into skill when you find out whether you were right or wrong. Michael Larson paused the VCR frame by frame.
That’s a brutally tight feedback loop.
Guess the pattern, check the tape, correct, repeat. Without feedback, “20 years of experience” is just one year lived 20 times. With it, a few months can beat a career.
3. Get close to the raw data.
Patterns live in the reps themselves, not in summaries of the reps.
Read the actual contracts, not the takeaways. Watch the actual game film, not the highlights. Look at the raw thing everyone else is too lazy or too important to look at. That’s where the structure is hiding.
4. Name your patterns and collect them like a psychopath.
The moment you can name a pattern, you own it.
High performers are secretly dirty little hoarders. They collect mental models, recurring setups, “oh, this is one of those situations” moments, and file them away.
Every time you catch a pattern, name it and add it to the shelf. Your library grows. Your glance gets faster.
5. Study across fields, not just your own.
The deepest patterns repeat everywhere.
The same shape shows up in biology, markets, relationships, and history but looks different each time.
The more domains you collect from, the more often you’ll walk into a “new” problem and think, “Wait, I’ve seen this before.” You haven’t. Not exactly. But your brain found the matching shape. That’s the superpower.
I once met a guy running a university. He was studying airlines like a madman. He took strategies from air travel and applied them to university. He became known as a crazy innovator. But he told me he wasn’t innovating at all.
He was just remixing together two industries that hadn’t been remixed before to produce outlier outcomes. Smart. Do that. Get naughty. Get weird.
6. Study history like a scholar with high pants and nerd glasses smoking a pipe
The best patterns lie in history.
Why? Because history doesn’t repeat but it rhymes. Most people are not looking for solutions from stuff that happened 500 years ago. So, if you do, you’ll look new and innovative. Ryan Holiday did this with stoicism.
He took philosophy from the Romans, that most people weren’t focusing on, and made it valuable and relevant for millennials and teeny boppers like me.
When you study history, you’ll see the most transformative patterns such as:
Money doesn’t go to the smartest. It goes to the most resourceful.
Governments don’t solve problems. They create them.
Poverty in some parts of the world will never die
Politicians benefit themselves, not you
There will always be war
Study history if you want to predict the patterns of the future. Human nature is so predictable and obvious, it’s scary.
Seeing patterns is not enough. You need this skill.
I’ve gotta let you down again because I am not your guru.
Pattern recognition isn’t enough. And it’s not the highest form of intelligence either. Sorry. Please don’t tell my mommy.
The highest form of intelligence is:
Pattern Recognition + Synthesis
One without the other only leads to stupidity and being fake-smart with a PhD. You must be able to see patterns and connect topics coherently.
This is how you create new ways of solving problems. As I’ve already proven, everyone can see patterns if they want to (many people refuse though).
But few people connect the patterns.
This is why I love writing online. Writing is just thinking. When you write about topics you see patterns and subconsciously join the dots as you write. I don’t even realize I’m doing it half the time. It’s a superpower.
The brutal truth
You either see patterns, or subconscious patterns run your life and make you a slave to circumstances and fate. There is no alternative. That’s gonna piss a lot of people off but so be it.
Pattern recognition is nothing new.
The most successful people in history have been studying patterns and joining the dots between them for centuries. They just rarely talk about it.
So consuming more information, listening to gurus, or spending 10,000 hours playing the violin to a bedroom audience of your cat and dog isn’t going to make you successful.
The brutal truth is patterns run the world. And those who consciously choose to see the patterns, join the dots, and use pattern interrupts when bad patterns take over… are the people who are guaranteed to win.
Everyone else will be controlled by hidden patterns spread by algorithmic newsfeeds that keep them stuck in an echo chamber of their own awesomeness and “I’m right and you’re wrong.”
The future belongs to pattern recognizers. Master pattern recognition.
