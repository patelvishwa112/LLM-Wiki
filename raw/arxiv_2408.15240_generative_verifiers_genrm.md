# Generative Verifiers: Reward Modeling as Next-Token Prediction

arXiv:2408.15240v3 [cs.LG] 22 Feb 2025 · ICLR 2025
Authors: Lunjun Zhang (DeepMind / Toronto), Arian Hosseini (DeepMind), Hritik Bansal (DeepMind / Mila), Mehran Kazemi (DeepMind / UCLA), Aviral Kumar (DeepMind / CMU), Rishabh Agarwal (DeepMind)
Project: https://sites.google.com/view/generative-reward-models
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15240

## Abstract (v3)

Verifiers or reward models are often used to enhance the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs). A common approach is the Best-of-N method, where N candidate solutions generated by the LLM are ranked by a verifier, and the best one is selected. While LLM-based verifiers are typically trained as discriminative classifiers to score solutions, they do not utilize the text generation capabilities of pretrained LLMs. To overcome this limitation, we instead propose training verifiers using the ubiquitous next-token prediction objective, jointly on verification and solution generation. Compared to standard verifiers, such generative verifiers (GenRM) can benefit from several advantages of LLMs: they integrate seamlessly with instruction tuning, enable chain-of-thought reasoning, and can utilize additional test-time compute via majority voting for better verification. We demonstrate that GenRM outperforms discriminative, DPO verifiers, and LLM-as-a-Judge, resulting in large performance gains with Best-of-N, namely 5% → 45.3% on algorithmic tasks and 73% → 93.4% on GSM8K. In easy-to-hard generalization settings, we observe improvements of 28% → 44.6% on MATH, and 37.9% → 53.5% on MMLU abstract algebra. Furthermore, we find that training GenRM with synthetic verification rationales is sufficient to pick out subtle errors on math problems. Finally, we demonstrate that GenRM scales favorably with model size and test-time compute.

## Problem setting

- Reasoning LLMs make confident logical mistakes; one error invalidates multi-step solutions.
- Best-of-N: sample N candidates from a generator; rank with a verifier/RM; pick top.
- Standard LLM verifiers are discriminative RMs (binary CE / scalar score via special cls token) — do not use generation capabilities (instruction tuning, CoT, test-time compute).
- LLM-as-a-Judge (prompt-only generative) has those advantages but underperforms trained verifiers on reasoning.

## Method: GenRM

### GenRM (direct)
- SFT next-token prediction to answer: "Is the answer correct (Yes/No)?"
- Verifier score at inference = P(Yes | problem, solution, prompt).
- Can jointly train on solution generation + verification under unified NTP (positive transfer; hard with DPO-style verifiers).

### GenRM-CoT
- SFT to emit verification chain-of-thought rationales before Yes/No.
- Rationales can be synthetic (e.g. from Gemini 1.0 Pro) and still catch subtle errors.
- Test-time: sample multiple CoT rationales; average P(Yes) across samples (majority-voting style use of inference compute).

### Motivating failure mode (GSM8K)
- Subtle wrong solution (missed "each" in streaming bundle pricing) scored ~0.999 by discriminative RM.
- GenRM-CoT steps through, flags incorrect intermediate step, ends No; majority-vote score ~0.0015.

## Selected results (paper claims)

| Setting | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Algorithmic (Last Letter Concat, BBH Word Sorting) Best-of-N | ~5% → 45.3% (large Δ vs baselines; near-oracle with GenRM-CoT voting on algorithmic) |
| GSM8K Best-of-N | 73% → 93.4% (Gemma2-9B GenRM-CoT verifying Gemini 1.0 Pro solutions; beats GPT-4 / Gemini 1.5 Pro in reported setup) |
| Easy-to-hard MATH500 | 28% → 44.6% (verifier trained only on GSM8K-level) |
| MMLU abstract algebra | 37.9% → 53.5% |
| Scaling | Favorable in dataset size, model capacity, and inference-time verification samples vs discriminative / LLM-as-Judge |

Also claims GenRM outperforms DPO-trained verifiers and self-consistency baselines in reported figures.

## Why generative verification helps

1. Same objective as pretrain/SFT — leverages LM strengths end-to-end.
2. Explicit verbalized critique before decision (process-level signal).
3. Natural test-time compute axis (more verification samples without more generator N only).
4. Unifies generate + verify training.

## Limitations / caveats for wiki use

- Results are Best-of-N with fixed generators; gains depend on candidate diversity and verifier calibration.
- GenRM-CoT needs verification rationales (synthetic OK per paper).
- Inference cost of multi-sample CoT verification can be large.
