I think Grok Bot is quickly going to become my most-used AI product. It’s rare that I am genuinely blown away by the product craft, design, and utility of a product all at once, but Grok Bot has passed that bar. I know it’s not unique in its functionality, but it takes several powerful AI capabilities and wraps it up in an extremely simple interface that makes it hard not to love.
In that way, it reminds me of the early days of Cursor: the interface was just a thin wrapper on VS Code, but it was enough to onboard an entire generation of engineers onto AI coding. I believe Grok Bot will have that same effect on all of knowledge work.
Here’s some of the things I think Grok Bot has done well that makes it special:
1. Cloud-first
It’s obvious to anyone using AI agents that cloud is the future. Devin was (too) early to this product hypothesis, but humans needed some time with local AI to first build trust as an active co-pilot. We’re now finally at the point where models are capable enough that they need much less human oversight to get things done, and the ability to seamlessly talk to the same agent from my phone and computer is amazing. I’ve been disappointed by the cloud experience in ChatGPT and Claude — it feels like the VMs are constantly dying, or lacking context I have locally, or don’t have an easy way to grab my auth/permissions for tasks.
2. iMessage-style interface
This one is controversial, because it could be argued that existing messaging apps (iMessage → Instinct or Slack → Claude Tag) already have the right interface for communicating with agents. However, as someone who is already overwhelmed by the amount of messages I have to respond to across texts, emails, and Slacks, it’s actually really nice to have a separate app with “channels” for focused workstreams/personas. It’s customizable enough to make it feel personal. The downside of this is that it can feel fragmented if you’re switching between multiple agent apps constantly and the context/memory isn’t shared across them.
One feature I love here is the swipe right to start a thread. I’m shocked Claude/ChatGPT haven’t done this yet in their consumer products! (Side note: Grok Bot definitely has Benji Taylor's whimsy written all over it.)
3. Computer Use
It’s so cool to watch my Grok Bots operate their own little computer! I’m not exactly sure why their computer use is so good - maybe the Grok 4.6 model is just really good at computer use?? Or they built some unique harness around it? This fills the gaps for me that MCPs don't cover. 
4. Secure API key inputs + Stripe Link
This + the Stripe Link integration makes me so much more likely to hand-off secure tasks like payments and logins to my Grok Bot.
5. First-class iOS app
Features like “Remote Control” or “Dispatch” have always felt clunky to me, and I hate how mobile has become an afterthought in knowledge work products. As someone who’s constantly traveling, I’ve enjoyed using the iOS app as a primary, first-class interface to get work done!
Also thought I’d share some early examples on how I’m using Grok Bots in my own workflows as a founder! If you think any of these might be useful to you, let me know and I’m happy to share any as a Template.
1. Finance Bro
Running a startup can often be a logistics and paperwork nightmare. You can now use Grok Bot to fill out all your random DE franchise tax, state company registrations, and other annoying paperwork! I have one Grok Bot named “Finance Bro” in charge of my company’s finances & paperwork. It maintains a Google Drive folder of all the source of truth documents/PDFs/spreadsheets, and uses Notion as a Task List to keep track of work in progress amongst its subagents.
I genuinely think this has saved me WEEKS of the most grueling, boring work digging through email attachments and filling out forms on ugly government websites.
2. Data Analytics, PM, Engineering
These are pretty classic use cases, but it’s been really nice to set up different Bots for each of these “functions” at the company and have them answer questions, do cleanup in Github/Notion, read Slack channels, and run automations to move work forward. Many other people have written about how to optimize Bots to do these things well, so I won’t spend more time on it here.
3. Ads, Influencer Marketing, SEO
Managing multiple marketing channels can be quite a hassle - I’m using separate Bots to monitor performance on each channel and do outreach when needed. For instance, the Influencer Marketing Bot manages a Notion CRM of potential influencers, scrapes TikTok/Instagram for new prospects, and sends out an automated email sequence once it’s ready. The Ads bot scrapes organic UGC content and uploads it to our creative management sheet for ad uploads. They're also connected to ground truth data from our MMP & ad platforms to monitor performance and make strategic bets.
4. Chief of Staff
I’ve never had an EA or Chief of Staff before, so this one feels pretty magical. I basically have my own Instinct-style personal assistant as one Bot. I have it fill out forms, pay invoices, surface important emails I might have missed, and manage my calendar. I haven’t had it start responding to emails for me automatically yet, but I may start doing that soon!
I’ve been wanting to connect multiple email inboxes to an AI agent for YEARS and none have supported until now. I do find it ironic that OpenAI finally launched it the same week as Grok Bot!
5. Fun things to do in SF
I have a Bot just named Fun that knows my hobbies & interests and pings me every Friday with a list of fun events happening in SF that are unique to me. Next step: connect to iMessage so it can coordinate with my friends to go to them together.
6. Shopping
It does research for me on products I want so I just have to look at pictures + prices to choose. Then it buys it for me and processes returns if I didn’t like it.
In conclusion, now is the best time to be a solo founder or working on a small team. Highly ambitious, ADHD-coded individuals can make SO much progress with a swarm of Bots!
