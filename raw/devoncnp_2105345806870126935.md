I - Why most getting rich advice is bullsh*t
A lot of it is focused around slow rich activities. Slow rich is a polite way of saying old (someone had to say it).
"If you put $500 a month into the S&P 500 for the next 40 years, you're going to be a millionaire." Yeah, that's technically true. But you also have to account for inflation. And then you also ask yourself, what the hell are you doing with your life in the meantime? Sacrificing your life for 40 years so that way you can finally enjoy your money once you're 65 years old?
A Dollar's Worth, by Visual Capitalist (2021), data from the BLS Consumer Price Index. Your $1,000,000 at 65 lives on the right-hand end of that curve.
That sounds awful. That sounds lame. I want to be rich now! I want to do stuff now! I want to make money now!
The other advice everybody has is work your way up the corporate ladder. You technically are going to make more money, especially if you work in a career for 20 years. Maybe you can get to the point where you're making $500,000 a year because you're a senior executive or something. But that is just selling your soul, kissing ass, and doing what you have to do to barely get by for years and years. If you're going to do it, you might as well just go get an investment banking job. At least you're going to get paid a lot.
And the last one is they always tell you to manifest yourself rich. Well I could just say "I'm going to get rich, I'm going to get rich, I'm going to get rich" all day and nothing would f*cking change in my life.
Instead you have to understand how people are actually getting rich in the modern day and age.
They're building a cash flowing business. They have high income skill sets that allow them to build a business, or spawn money out of thin air.
So if you want to get rich, you have to be in a better vehicle. You have to be in something that will actually allow you to make the amount of money you want to make. The issue with most people is they're stuck in a career that will take them forever to ever get rich. In order to get rich before you're old, you must find ways of making money a lot faster.
What are these opportunities? YouTube (which by the way is how I made my first million dollars). Being a social media influencer (on camera or not). Having a marketing agency. Affiliate marketing, where you're selling somebody else's product and getting a cut. Day trading or crypto. I'm not saying this is the best way of getting rich, because you're dependent on the markets. But it's a way to get to the point where you spawn money out of thin air. And building a boring business. Landscaping is a huge one. I have many friends (surprisingly) that make multiple millions of dollars a year with a landscaping business, and they're just really good at landscaping. Now, it took them seven years to get to that point, but it's still significantly faster than 40.
You're not going to get rich renting out your time.
– Naval Ravikant
Nobody gets unconsciously rich renting out their time.
You need to be in control of your money through the skill sets that you have.
Stop saying "I'm being realistic." Say "I'm in the wrong vehicle."
II - Why 100 reps beat a four-year degree
The better you get at being a YouTuber, the more views you're going to get. The more views you get, the more money you're going to make. The more money you make, the better videos you can make. And the better videos you make, the more views you get. It becomes this constant feedback cycle.
The beauty of these businesses is you get a very fast feedback cycle on whether or not what you're doing is working. That's why most jobs just pay you a 9 to 5 wage. The business owner is getting the feedback cycle. The market's getting the feedback cycle. You're renting out your time.
So you have inputs, and then you have outputs. You want to close the gap of getting outputs as fast as possible. This is what is known as feedback. Once you see the outputs, you are now getting feedback. And the more feedback you get, the faster you're going to learn and the better you're going to become at the things you do. What we call the thing in between the inputs and the outputs is your process. Your system.
You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems.
– James Clear, Atomic Habits
Inputs, process, outputs, feedback. This is how feedback loops work and how systems and processes get better.
I could write 100 of these articles in the next two or three months and have more evidence, more experience of marketing, than someone would have from a four-year college degree.
The feedback loop is so damn slow going to school. When you put something out, you get immediate feedback. I get to see what the comments are saying. I get to see what the views are saying. I get to see the engagement curve, when you guys fall off and when you want to watch a moment over and over again.
These opportunities allow you to fulfill that feedback loop significantly faster, so you get way more repetitions than the average person. And when you get more repetitions than the average person, you start to be able to make more money.
You have to view success as the ability to understand data points and recognize the patterns. If I had a graph of a bunch of random dots, it just looks like a bunch of random dots. But if the x-axis is the money you made and the y-axis is the video you made, and you do lots of data points, you can see the videos you put the most amount of time into. The videos you put the most thought into. The more refined, developed ideas. The ones you copied and emulated from other people's success. And you're thinking to yourself: oh wow, they get results like this. So if I want a result like that, I need to make more videos that are over here.
The way you do that is through pattern recognition. The way you get pattern recognition is through looking at lots of data points and getting lots of feedback. And that's why people are getting so rich. With the internet you can look at so many data points so quickly. It gets to a point where you're not even thinking about how much money you're making. That's the unconscious part.
Reps = data points = pattern = money.
For the entirety of this week, here's what I want you to do: anytime you pull your card out and buy something, ask yourself how they got you to buy it. Was it the quality? Was it convenient? Did it give you an identity? Every time you buy is a chance to see how they took your money, and how you can replicate it.
So that's the opportunist. You need to jump on things. You need to see the things that can actually change your life and be somebody who jumps on these vehicles, rather than staying stagnant and comfortable in your current reality.

IV - Personality LARPing
Which brings me to my next point, and I love this concept. I invented it with my team. It's called Personality LARPing.
You want to live-action roleplay the reality you want to live. If you want to be somebody who's a badass who's making a ton of money and traveling the world, then start roleplaying it. Start acting as if you're that person. You need to start pretending that you're that rich dude that's able to travel the world and do whatever he wants every single day.
This is also known as the alter ego. There's a guy that wrote a good book about this, The Alter Ego Effect by Todd Herman. What you're doing is wearing a mask. You're pretending to be the superhero. You're pretending to be this badass business guy in your day to day life.
It sounds corny. But what that does, neurologically and scientifically, is it actually gets you to start acting different. It starts to give you different personality traits. Because your personal reality is just the reality you've created based off your habits and your behaviors and the way you've identified yourself.
There's fifty years of psychology behind that. Daryl Bem called it self-perception theory in 1972: you decide who you are by watching what you do. Your brain watches you skip the gym three days in a row and files you under "not a gym person." The costume isn't fake. The costume is the only evidence your brain has.
We are what we pretend to be, so we must be careful about what we pretend to be.
– Kurt Vonnegut, Mother Night
So you need to ask yourself: who is the person I need to become?

Which is actually backwards. Instead, they need to focus on becoming the rich person first, then what that person believes, and then from those beliefs and that identity you come up with your own actions.
Because it's very easy to hear "oh rich people read and learn all the time," and go read and learn a bunch of sh*t that isn't going to change your life.
Identity, then beliefs, then actions.
So you need to start acting as if you are somebody who's successful. You need to wear the mask. You need to pretend that you are somebody who is successful. So much so that it just becomes your new norm. Because once you've pretended to be somebody for long enough, your brain starts to rewire and you start to act completely differently. That's where it goes unconscious. You're not acting anymore. You just are.
Intention, action, behavior, habit, practice, second nature, who you are.
You don't become rich and then start acting rich. You act rich, and then you become it.
V - Disappear and lock in for six months
So how does this actually work? You change your environment.
Some of you are reading this thinking, I already knew that. But if you actually knew it, you wouldn't still be in the same room, would you?
Your environment is everything when it comes to who you are. Reading this right now on your phone or your computer, you're reminded of this version of yourself, because you've looked at this device so many times. If you look around your room, all these things reinforce who you are right now. It's not until you change your environment (until you shift the location you're in) that you finally start to see the world differently, rebuild new habits, and completely change the person you are.
Because right now (especially if you live in your hometown) the parents around you remind you of who you are. Your friends remind you of who you are. The color of the walls, the smell in the air, reminds you of who you are.
All of it votes for the old version of you.
There's a study on this. Wendy Wood had people log their days and found about 45% of what they did was repeated in the same place, almost every day. Nearly half your life is a rerun.
So you have to move. You have to change your location. If I were to project one lifestyle change onto people that allows them to genuinely get rich, it's move out of your parents' house. Because when you don't, you're constantly reminded of who you already were in the past.

The fastest way to become the person you want to be is to put yourself in a situation where you have no choice but to become them.

Which is great, because it was a new start. A new life. I didn't necessarily align with all the people in Idaho, and I had a tough time staying there for a long time. So the last year and a half, I've literally changed my environment every single week, because I believe in this part so much. It allowed me to turbocharge the change in my life, because I'm no longer tied to a very specific person. And in this process I've 3x'd the amount of money I make, just by constantly traveling and constantly changing my environment. I'm not anchored to any belief pattern or place.
Even when I did stay in a place for a certain amount of time, I would leave and go to a mastermind event, go meet other people that are building stuff. They would help rewire and shift my beliefs and help me see the world in a completely different way.
Two psychologists at the University of Bath tested this. The nudge worked on people who had moved house in the last three months, and did nothing for people who hadn't. Call it the 90-day window. Use it.
So it's just this: you need to disappear and lock in for six months, and you will completely change your life.

Where do you live? Are you still living in the current zip code you live in now? Are you still living in the same house? Probably not. Think no limits. Anything is possible.
What car do you drive? Are you driving a Honda Civic? A G-Wagon? A Lambo? Cars aren't everything. But it helps you understand something tangible. For me, I travel. I have a sports car, but I don't even have time to drive it because I'm never home.
What's your daily routine? Are you up in the morning grinding it out the first part of the day? Then you go to the gym, because you're somebody who really values the gym. Then lunch at a restaurant, a cappuccino, salsa dancing at 3pm? What are you doing in your day-to-day life?
