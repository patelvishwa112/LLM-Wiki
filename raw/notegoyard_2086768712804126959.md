Anthropic just did something most labs never do - they shipped a model and told you upfront it's not their smartest one
That's not marketing modesty-It's the actual strategy

What shipped, July 24
Opus 5 - new flagship-tier model, live on every Anthropic platform same day $5 / $25 per million tokens [ input/output ] - unchanged from Opus 4.8, same price, better output New default on Claude Max, strongest option on Claude Pro Comes "close" to Fable 5 - Anthropic's actual smartest model - on most tasks, at half the cost
This is the fourth model Anthropic shipped in under two months, following Mythos 5, Fable 5, and Sonnet 5 in June
release cadence itself is part of the story - nobody in this market gets to sit on a model for a year anymore
The part everyone's skipping past - the effort dial
You can now tell Opus 5 how hard to think
Low, medium, high effort - same model, different compute spend
Low effort - most of the intelligence, a fraction of the tokens High effort - closer to Fable tier reasoning when the task actually needs it Fast mode - runs 2.5x speed at 2x price, for when latency matters more than depth
This is the real shift
Intelligence stopped being one fixed dial you're locked into by model choice
It's now priced like compute - spend more when the task earns it, spend less when it doesn't
Anthropic's own framing backs this up: the charts they published for this launch emphasize performance at a given cost, not peak performance alone
That's a different pitch than every previous flagship release, which was always "look how smart it is" This one is "look how smart it is per dollar"
Why "downgrade" is the wrong word - and the actual play
Anthropic isn't hiding that Fable 5 is smarter
betting most of the money in AI isn't spent at the frontier
It's spent in the middle - the daily coding task, the internal doc summary, the agent loop that runs 400 times a day and needs to not bankrupt you on call 401
Fable 5 - for the slice of tasks that genuinely need frontier reasoning, including autonomous work that runs for days unsupervised Opus 5 - for the overwhelming majority of daily work, at half the burn rate, with a dial to push harder when a specific task calls for it
That's Anthropic finally pricing for how people actually use these models, instead of making every summarization task pay frontier rates by default
timing isn't accidental either
Fable 5 launched controversial - a temporary U.S. export control suspension in June after researchers found ways around its safeguards, plus real pushback from business customers about how many tokens it burned through on ordinary tasks
Opus 5 is Anthropic's answer to that second complaint, directly
The safety number nobody's talking about
Opus 5 posted a 2.3 in Anthropic's internal behavioral audit - the lowest misconduct rate of any current Claude model
More adherent to Claude's Constitution than Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, or even Fable 5 itself
It still trails Mythos 5 on biological research and offensive cybersecurity - on purpose, not by accident
During testing like OSS Fuzz, Opus 5 found vulnerabilities about as well as Mythos 5 did, but was noticeably weaker at turning those vulnerabilities into working exploits
Anthropic isn't racing those specific capabilities in a model this widely deployed, and says so directly rather than letting the benchmark chart speak for it
Two smaller releases shipped alongside Opus 5, both useful if you're building with it:
Mid-conversation tool changes - developers can now change which tools Claude has access to mid session without invalidating the prompt cache Automatic fallbacks - requests flagged by safety classifiers on Opus 5 or Fable 5 can now auto route to another model instead of just getting blocked
Why this actually matters for anyone building right now
If you're running agent loops, batch pipelines, or anything that fires hundreds of API calls a day, this is the model that makes that math work
Fable 5 for the hard 5%, Opus 5 at low effort for the other 95%, same architecture, same account, one dial between them instead of a hard model switch
model race stopped being purely about who's smartest
It's increasingly about who's smartest per dollar, per task, per day - and Anthropic just made that the actual headline of a launch instead of burying it in a footnote
