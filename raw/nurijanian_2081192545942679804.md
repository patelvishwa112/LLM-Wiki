# Decision memos: top-down + bottom-up on one page

Author: George from prodmgmt.world @nurijanian
Posted: 2026-07-26T01:39:04.000Z
URL: https://x.com/nurijanian/status/2081192545942679804

A memo or decision document can change a complex problem into a clear directive for a team. Much of the work involves dividing the problem into smaller components, creating more options and structure. The document can become lengthy, and a final decision may be delayed.

One method to finalize the document is to approach the problem from two directions.

First, work from general principles to specific details (top-down).
Second, work from specific evidence to broader conclusions (bottom-up).

Then, compare the results of these two approaches on a single page.
The areas where the two approaches align form the basis of the directive. Discrepancies between the two approaches should be documented rather than resolved prematurely.

**The process**

1. **State the decision.** Write the decision you need to make, who must approve it, and the deadline. "Pricing" is a subject. "Should we switch to per-seat pricing before renewal season begins, and who will approve this change?" is a decision.
2. **Structure the decision from general to specific.** Before examining data, write the possible answers that logic suggests. These are the few potential solutions if circumstances were ideal. Considering these possibilities without looking at evidence ensures objectivity.
3. **Organize the evidence from specific to general.** Group the available information: customer support tickets, conversations with users, product usage data, sales meeting notes, and comments made by engineers during daily stand-ups. Let each group present its own findings. Do not force the evidence to fit a preferred conclusion.
4. **Combine the findings on a single page.** Place the results from the top-down and bottom-up analyses next to each other. Note where the logical possibilities and the evidence align, where they conflict, and which potential solutions lack supporting evidence.
5. **Present the conclusion first, with a condition for changing it.** State the decision in the first sentence. Provide the supporting evidence below. Clearly explain any disagreements or conflicts rather than omitting them. Conclude with a single sentence identifying the specific evidence that would lead to a different decision.

This method goes beyond the "Pyramid Principle," which often stops at presenting the answer first. The crucial work is in steps 2 through 4: conducting the top-down and bottom-up analyses independently, and then identifying the discrepancies between them. Without this process, simply stating the conclusion first risks presenting a predetermined outcome without thorough investigation.

**Why this is harder than it looks**

Without a requirement to reach a conclusion, each new piece of information increases complexity. People create tools that divide problems into smaller pieces, allowing this complexity to grow. These tools do not indicate when to stop increasing complexity and reach a resolution for both sides.

The difficult decision is determining which findings are considered consistent and which are considered contradictory. Two findings that point in a similar direction either form the basis of your decision or are aspects you are reconciling. This choice is made by the individual, not by the process.

I believe most product managers already possess this decision-making ability. What they need is a structured format that allows this judgment to be clearly articulated in writing.

**One memo versus ten**

AI is changing how work is done. More drafts, offering more choices, more data breakdowns, and more conclusions to consider. This means more information is available for each decision and more decisions can be made each week.

With this increased volume, it becomes difficult to process both the initial information and the final summary mentally. A blank template does not solve this problem. The challenge is not simply filling in required fields. Late at night before a review, you still need to prevent the overall summary from overriding the detailed analysis.

An AI agent can manage this process. It consistently performs the same two analytical steps. Instead of making changes, it highlights areas of agreement and disagreement. It provides a summary-first page that can be submitted. The actual discussion or meeting remains your responsibility.
