## Referenced paper

Source: the thread announces arXiv:2610.02140, "Finetuning with Sampling: SFT Learns Better Than You Think"
by Aayush Karan, Sitan Chen and Yilun Du (Harvard), submitted 1 Oct 2026 (paper read 2026-10-24;
project page `aakaran.github.io/finetuning_with_sampling`, code `github.com/aakaran/finetuning-with-sampling`).

**Method — projection sampling.** The paper leaves the SFT objective untouched and shapes the data
instead. Given expert traces from an off-policy policy `q` and a base model `p`, the target is the
information projection of `p` onto the set `C` of traces semantically equivalent to the expert trace
(correct final answer, or same factual content under an LLM grader): `p_C ∝ p(x)·1(x ∈ C)`. That is the
closest distribution to the base model that still carries the expert information. Because rejection
sampling from it fails when the base model cannot produce the answer at all, the paper samples it with
Metropolis–Hastings: initialize at the expert trace, propose information-preserving mutations (a
continuation prompt that must reach the same final answer), and accept using the likelihood ratio.
KL(data policy ‖ base) is provably non-increasing in every MCMC step. For LLMs they use block-wise
"projection sampling" — block size B=32, max length T=1856, N_MCMC=10 steps per block — reusing the
prior work's power-sampling implementation. Swapping whenever a higher-likelihood candidate is generated
(two forward passes per iteration would otherwise be needed) shifts more aggressively but still reduces
KL monotonically. Information preservation is checked empirically: boosted data keeps 94.33% (Qwen,
chemistry), 93.94% (Olmo, chemistry), 95.33% (math) and 95.86% (medical) of original accuracy.

**Results (Table 1).** Chemistry on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct: base 0.343, SFT 0.618, rewrite-SFT (0th-order
MCMC) 0.613, OPSD 0.618, sampling SFT 0.660 (+31.7 points over base, +4.2 over OPSD). Prior-capability
average: base 0.597, SFT 0.520, rewrite 0.558, OPSD 0.568, sampling SFT 0.586 — the least forgetting of
any arm at −1.10 points average, and MMLU is unchanged (0.692 vs base 0.687). Math on Qwen2.5-3B:
sampling SFT 0.495 on MATH(3,4,5) vs GRPO 0.457 and UFT 0.470; 0.582 on MATH500 (+33.7 over the 0.245
base), 0.782 GSM8K. GRPO initialized from the sampling-SFT checkpoint is the strongest model overall —
0.545 MATH(3,4,5), 0.652 MATH500, 0.830 GSM8K. Medical on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct: sampling SFT 0.458 vs
OPSD 0.466 and plain SFT 0.448, but it recovers 16.3 of the
24.4 points that plain SFT loses on average across prior capabilities (0.516 vs 0.353; base 0.597). Olmo-3-7B-Instruct on chemistry: sampling SFT 0.583 vs OPSD
0.597, with the best prior-capability average (0.617 vs base 0.601).

**Two controls.** Boosted data is model-specific: fine-tuning Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on data boosted for
Olmo drops chemistry to 0.5733, below vanilla SFT's 0.618. A 50/50 mix of boosted and original data
lands at 0.6214, between the two. Learning goes beyond sharpening: pass@k for sampling SFT stays above
both the base model and OPSD at high k, so the model is not just re-weighting what it already knew.
